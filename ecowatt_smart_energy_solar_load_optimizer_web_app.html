Environmental & Grid Sustainability Impact

In thermal-dominated power grids, every 1 kWh of wasted electricity requires ~1.15 kWh of generation at the power plant due to transmission and distribution (T&D) losses. By combining demand-side load scheduling, power factor correction (reducing line current I and I²R copper losses), and localized rooftop solar PV, buildings significantly relieve peak transformer stress and cut greenhouse gas emissions.

1. Core Electrical Engineering Formulas

A. Active Energy Consumption (kWh):

E = (Pwatts × Quantity × Hours × 30) / 1000  [kWh/month]

B. Apparent Power (kVA) & Power Factor:

S (kVA) = P (kW) / cos φ   |   Q (kVAR) = √(S² − P²)

C. Shunt Capacitor Bank Sizing:

Qc = P × [tan(cos⁻¹ φ₁) − tan(cos⁻¹ φ₂)]  [kVAR]

D. Copper Loss Reduction Factor:

% Loss Reduction = [1 − (cos φ₁ / cos φ₂)²] × 100%

E. Carbon Footprint Offset (CEA India Baseline):

CO₂ (kg) = Energy Saved (kWh) × 0.82 kg CO₂/kWh

2. Project Abstract & Scope

Abstract: Rising electricity demand in academic campuses and residential buildings contributes significantly to peak grid stress and carbon emissions. EcoWatt is a browser-based Electrical Engineering decision-support tool that eliminates expensive IoT sensor barriers by providing deterministic load auditing, reactive power capacitor bank sizing, and rooftop solar PV offset simulation.

Honest Limitations (Simulated vs. Measured Data): This software uses user-entered nameplate ratings and standard solar irradiance averages rather than real-time CT/PT hardware telemetry. Therefore, results serve as pre-installation engineering feasibility estimates.

Future Scope: Integration with ESP32 + PZEM-004T energy meters via MQTT/REST APIs for live telemetry and automated relay switching of capacitor banks (APFC panel).

Top Viva Questions & Model Answers

Q1. Why did you choose a software/web-based project instead of a hardware prototype?

Before installing physical solar panels or Automatic Power Factor Correction (APFC) capacitor banks, electrical engineers must conduct an energy audit and sizing feasibility study. A web-based simulator allows campuses and households to model savings, size components accurately, and identify phantom loads at zero hardware cost.

Q2. How does improving Power Factor save energy if capacitors don't reduce active power (kW) of the appliance?

While an induction motor still consumes the same active power (P = V·I·cos φ), improving cos φ reduces the total line current (I = P / (V·cos φ)) drawn from the transformer and distribution cables. Lower current directly reduces transmission/distribution copper losses (I²R losses), improves voltage regulation, and eliminates utility kVAh/PF penalties.

Q3. How does your project directly contribute to UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)?

It directly supports SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy) by facilitating rooftop solar PV sizing and improving end-use energy efficiency (Target 7.3), and SDG 13 (Climate Action) by quantifying CO₂ emission reductions using grid emission factors (0.82 kg CO₂/kWh).

Q4. What is Performance Ratio (PR) in your Solar PV formula?

Performance Ratio (typically 0.75–0.85) accounts for real-world losses between DC panel nameplate output and usable AC output—including inverter efficiency (~96%), high cell temperature derating, dust/soiling, and DC/AC cable losses.

Q5. How is data stored in this web application without a backend server?

The application uses the browser's localStorage API to persist custom appliance inventories and tariff settings locally, ensuring fast offline execution during demonstrations without paid cloud databases or APIs.
